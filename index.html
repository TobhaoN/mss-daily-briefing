TOP STORY

Stellantis moves to stop dealers advertising rock-bottom prices

Stellantis is the latest OEM to impose minimum advertised pricing (MAP), setting a floor beneath which Jeep, Ram, Dodge and Chrysler dealers can't advertise a vehicle. With Stellantis vehicles discounted roughly three times deeper than the industry average, the automaker is trying to protect brand integrity and shift competition away from headline price toward experience — a direct hit to digital-first, price-comparison shopping.

Volkswagen to ramp up restructuring after profit warning

Days after cutting its 2026 margin outlook to at most 1%, VW's core-brand chief said the automaker will accelerate its performance program as workers staged nationwide protests. VW plans a further 50,000 job cuts amid US tariffs, Chinese pressure and the drag of faster-than-expected demand for lower-margin EVs.

Volvo Cars names Skoda chief Klaus Zellmer as new CEO

Geely-owned Volvo will install industry veteran Klaus Zellmer as president and CEO by October 2027 to revive sales hit by tariffs, soft EV demand and high costs. He inherits a 13-model 2030 plan and the tricky task of separating Western and Chinese tech to keep selling in the US.

What it means for us: leadership churn and a model-blitz plan means shifting inventory mixes — a reason for Volvo dealers to lean on vAuto Live Market View to price and stock the incoming lineup against real demand rather than guesswork.

The risk of rushing AI agents & what dealers can do about it

Industry analyst David Spisak argues dealers are adopting AI agents faster than they can govern them, exposing stores to legal and FTC risk. He points to cautionary cases — including a dealership chatbot talked into "selling" an $81,000 Tahoe — and a widening gap between AI "haves" and "have-nots," stressing that real readiness needs data guardrails, human oversight and infrastructure.

Dealership Marketing & Advertising

Stellantis MAP rules reshape how dealers can advertise price

Under Stellantis's new minimum advertised pricing, dealers can't publish fire-sale numbers — though factory "bonus cash" incentives are untouched, and dealers can still negotiate once a shopper is engaged. Several dealers told the WSJ they'll pivot advertising toward service and experience, competing on price only after the lead is in the door.

What it means for us: if advertised price is capped, the lead's first impression shifts to marketing and merchandising — a clear case for Dealer.com (DDC) to carry the experience-and-value story and Autotrader/KBB to keep those units visible and credibly priced.
