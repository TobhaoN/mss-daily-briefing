Dealer payment quotes may be the FTC's next transparency target

A guest commentary in Automotive News warns that the gap between the monthly payment a dealer quotes and the terms a lender actually approves is emerging as the next pricing-transparency flashpoint for the FTC. It notes the 97 dealerships that received warning letters in March and a group facing potentially ~$80 million in fines, arguing enforcement urgency is rising across the industry.

What it means for us: Expect dealers to ask this week how to defend their desking and payment presentation. That is a direct opening for Dealertrack Deal Central and a clean digital deal jacket — consistent, documented figures from quote to contract are exactly the paper trail that answers an FTC transparency question.

Automotive Market

U.S. auto sales fell 6.3% in August as hybrids cushion the dip

Preliminary MarkLines data pegs August 2026 U.S. new-vehicle sales at about 1,382,819 units, down 6.3% year over year, partly reflecting one fewer selling day. Strong hybrid demand offset broader softness, while renewed U.S.–Canada trade friction surfaced as a fresh concern.

What it means for us: A softer, hybrid-skewed market is a vAuto conversation — dealers need live demand and turn signals to stock the hybrid mix that is actually moving rather than sitting on slow EV inventory. Pair it with KBB valuations to keep trade-in offers defensible as the mix shifts.

🎯 Talk track"With sales down 6.3% and hybrids carrying the month, ask the dealer what their hybrid days-supply looks like — that's where vAuto's live market data earns its keep this week."

Source: MarkLines · Sept. 2, 2026

A half-price Polestar comes with a big asterisk as China rule bites

The Polestar 4 is selling near half price — roughly $31,400 after a $25,000 incentive — because the Chinese-owned brand must exit the U.S. after 2026 under a new federal "connected vehicle" security rule barring certain Chinese and Russian-linked software. Cox Automotive's Stephanie Valdez Streaty is quoted noting price is the biggest EV barrier and that such discounts make EVs accessible.

What it means for us: Dealers will ask how to price and appraise a brand that's being wound down — a textbook vAuto and KBB question, since residual and reconditioning risk on an orphaned brand needs data, not guesswork, before they take one in trade.

Source: The New York Times · Aug. 31, 2026 (updated Sept. 1)

Three looming questions as Trump's MPG rollback nears

The Detroit News reports the Trump administration is expected to finalize sharply relaxed fuel-economy standards for cars and light trucks within days, with several open questions about how the new rules will treat EV credits and powertrain assumptions. The piece frames uncertainty for automakers' long-horizon product planning.

What it means for us: Powertrain-mix uncertainty makes stocking decisions riskier, which is exactly when vAuto's demand-based stocking guidance matters — dealers will want help deciding how much ICE vs. hybrid vs. EV to carry as incentives and mandates shift under them.

Source: The Detroit News · Sept. 2, 2026
