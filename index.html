Automotive Market

Rapid EV uptake drives a split in the US fuel market

Reuters columnist Gavin Maguire argues EV adoption is creating a two-tier "fuel" market: gasoline costs track global oil, while charging costs track local electricity rates. National EV charging averages about $1.56/gallon-equivalent versus $4.43 for gas, but the state-by-state spread for charging is wide — from ~$1.14 in Wyoming to ~$3.45 in Hawaii.

What it means for us: Geography now drives total cost of ownership as much as MSRP. A dealer can use vAuto and KBB data to reframe EV desking around local "cents-per-mile" savings for in-market shoppers — a concrete story for markets with cheap power, and a caution for high-rate states.

Dealership Marketing & Advertising

FTC issues more guidance on car-dealer advertising rules

New FTC staff FAQs (published Sept. 15, following warning letters to 97 dealer groups) say the advertised price must be the price any consumer can actually pay — every dealer-required fee included, excluding only charges a government requires the buyer to pay directly. The rules apply across websites, third-party listings, social, signs, calls and texts.

What it means for us: Every price a dealer publishes online is now compliance-sensitive. This is the moment to show how Dealer.com and Autotrader listing controls keep the "most prominent price" accurate and consistent across channels — an FTC-defensible merchandising story competitors will scramble to match.

Banks warn AI shopping bots raise scam, fraud and data-privacy risks

A group of banks (NatWest, Bank of America, ING, Capital One and others) cautioned that AI agents shopping on consumers' behalf outpace current consumer protections, and proposed disclosure when an agent is involved, more decision transparency and stronger data safeguards. John Lewis reported agent-originated searches jumped to 2.5% from 0.3% a year earlier.

What it means for us: "Agentic commerce" is coming to car shopping — buyers will soon send AI agents to price and configure vehicles. Dealer.com and Autotrader need clean, machine-readable pricing (see the FTC story) so a dealer's inventory is what agents surface accurately, not misquote.
