Automotive Market

New-vehicle affordability declines as ATP hits record $51,109

The Cox Automotive/Moody's Analytics Vehicle Affordability Index showed October affordability slipping as Kelley Blue Book put the average transaction price at a record $51,109, up 1.3% from September. The typical monthly payment rose to $783, the highest since December 2023, and buyers needed 35.3 weeks of median income to cover an average new vehicle. Affordability is still better than a year ago, helped by lower rates, higher incomes and bigger incentives.

What it means for us: this is the number that makes the vAuto and Manheim conversation easy. When the new-car payment prices people out, the store's gross has to come from used acquisition and reconditioning speed, not from the new-car desk. Expect dealers to ask what their sub-$35K used supply looks like.

Talk track: “At a $783 average payment, your next ten deals are coming off the used line. Where are you sourcing them, and what are you paying for them?”

CBT News · Nov. 18, 2026

Used vehicle sales jump 12% in November

AutoVision's monthly inventory analysis showed used sales up nearly 12% from mid-October to mid-November, with every age group's top ten models gaining. Late-model sales rose 12.2% and vehicles twelve years and older rose 19.3%. Supply went the other way: arrivals of the top ten models fell 21.2% and one-to-three-year-old inventory tightened 23.6%, while days to turn slowed as stores sold through older units.

What it means for us: demand is up and fresh supply is down, which is the exact squeeze Stockwave and KBB Instant Cash Offer are built for. The service-lane and private-party acquisition pitch stops being a nice-to-have this month and becomes the only place to find a late-model car at a number that works.

OpenLane sees renewed momentum in off-lease inventory

Speaking at Used Car Week in San Diego, OpenLane's Justin Zane said year-over-year off-lease volume grew 45%, with mid-to-high-teens growth even after stripping out one large customer win. He also said dealers are pressing hardest on two things: faster titles and more data to cut days on the lot, and that EV and hybrid searches are widening.

What it means for us: OpenLane is competing with Manheim on exactly the two complaints dealers bring us — title speed and days to turn. That makes Dealertrack's title workflow and Manheim's data a paired conversation, not two separate line items, and it is worth asking a dealer which marketplace is actually getting their off-lease buys right now.

TrueCar is bringing back its price-distribution curve, which shows shoppers where a given price sits against what other buyers actually paid, along with a Savings Certificate. Cox Automotive Ecosystem

Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index: Mid-November 2026 Trends

Wholesale used-vehicle values held flat through the first half of November, with the Manheim index at 206.8, unchanged from October and up 0.7% year over year. Non-adjusted prices eased 0.7%, a normal seasonal move. Wholesale supply tightened to 27.9 days, and compact cars and EVs outperformed while luxury, SUVs and pickups stayed under last year.

What it means for us: flat wholesale with tight supply is the cleanest setup all quarter for a vAuto pricing-to-market conversation, because a dealer holding aged units cannot blame a falling market for the gap. Bring the segment split — compacts and EVs up, trucks and luxury down — into the appraisal discussion.
