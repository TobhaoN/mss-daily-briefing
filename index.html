Automotive Market

JD Power forecasts new-vehicle sales to rise 1.2% in Nov.

JD Power and GlobalData have November new-vehicle sales tracking up 1.2% on a selling-day-adjusted basis, with retail at 996,000 units. Average transaction price is forecast at a November record $47,154 and the average monthly payment at a record $840, up 4.2%. Total SAAR comes in at 15.7 million and retail SAAR at 12.5 million, both down slightly. Dealer inventories are up nearly 7% year over year at 2.4 million units, incentive spend rose about 6% to $3,589, and 12.8% of new loans now run 84 months or longer with roughly 30% of buyers upside down on a trade.

What it means for us: 30% negative equity is the number that sells KBB Instant Cash Offer and vAuto appraisal discipline, because a store that misses the trade by $1,500 cannot buy that deal back on the front end. Expect dealers to ask how to structure around payment, and the honest answer sits in the trade, not the incentive.

Talk track: “A record $840 payment and a third of your customers underwater. You're not winning these deals on the new car, you're winning them on what you put on the trade.”

Dealership & Retail Tech

Vitu warns of growing title fraud threat facing used-car dealers

At Used Car Week, Vitu's Dave Spencer laid out how title delays and title fraud are hitting used-car operations, and argued dealers should weigh title availability and expected turnaround as part of the acquisition decision itself. He pointed to Vitu's National Title Exchange delivering titles in three business days or less, sometimes same or next day, and cited a retailer moving vehicles from acquisition to retail-ready in about 30 hours.

What it means for us: title speed is now being sold as an acquisition filter, not back-office plumbing, and that is Dealertrack's home turf. Worth asking a dealer what their average days-to-title actually is, because most cannot answer it and floorplan is being burned while they guess.

Cox Automotive Ecosystem

Buick dealer inventory climbs again in October 2026

Cox Automotive data drawn from vAuto Live Market View put the national new-vehicle days' supply at 87 in October, up from 83 in September. The spread by brand is wide: Jeep at 159 days, Ram 148, Dodge 147, Buick 136 and Lincoln 130. About half of available new inventory is advertised at $50,000 or less, while vehicles priced between $30,000 and $40,000 sat at 74 days and those above $80,000 at roughly 110.

What it means for us: vAuto Live Market View is the number dealers will be quoted back at them all quarter, and PCs covering Stellantis, Buick or Lincoln stores should walk in already knowing the brand's supply. It reframes the pricing-to-market conversation from theory to a specific gap between that store and an 87-day national average.

US advertisers spent $70.7M on ChatGPT ads in October, AdClarity estimates

AdClarity, the ad intelligence platform run by BIScience, estimates US advertisers put $70.7 million into ChatGPT ads in October, up 127% from $31.1 million in April. Global spend across 24 tracked countries came to $110.6 million, with the US at roughly 64% of it and the UK nearly tripling since June. Programming and developer software was the largest ad category. These are third-party estimates, not OpenAI figures.

What it means for us: a channel went from zero to a $70 million month while most dealers were still arguing about whether AI search matters, and Pinewood already sells ChatGPT ads inside a DMS marketing tool. Dealer.com and our advertising team will get asked about this, and the credible first move is still inventory data quality rather than buying into an unproven format.

Talk track: “There's real money going into ChatGPT ads already. Before you chase the buy, make sure your inventory data is good enough that the AI would pick you for free.”

Why dealers should own their data before adding AI

QoreAI's Todd Smith told CBT at Used Car Week that 25 years of single-use tools left dealer data scattered across the DMS, CRM and marketing platforms, and urged dealers to consolidate it somewhere they own rather than with a vendor. He warned against uploading customer lists into public AI tools, framed vendor agreements as AI contracts, and said dealers should budget AI like payroll — he expects roughly $5,000 a month per store against a $94 billion industry payroll line.

What it means for us: "who owns the data" is about to be a standard objection in every Cox renewal, and we should be able to answer it plainly rather than deflect. It also argues for the connected-platform case, because the dealer consolidating data themselves is doing by hand what VinSolutions, vAuto and Xtime already share.
