TOP STORY · Cox Automotive Ecosystem

Auto Market Weekly Summary: the cost of money is now a bigger part of the deal

Cox Chief Economist Jeremy Robb says interest on a typical new-vehicle loan is up 79% since 2019 to about $10.1K, and up 96% to about $10.9K on a used vehicle, while average used down payments have fallen to nearly zero. That pushes all-in cost up 37% for new and 43% for used versus 2019, faster than the roughly 30% rise in vehicle prices. Mid-October Manheim values also fell 2% before seasonal adjustment, a steeper drop than usual.

What it means for us: Customers are shopping a payment, not a price, and many are walking in with no cash down. That changes how we sell Dealertrack F&I and Deal Central: lead with payment transparency up front, and lean on KBB ICO because trade equity is now doing the job the down payment used to do.

Talk track: "Interest on a typical used deal is up 96% since 2019 and down payments are basically gone, so your customer is shopping a payment, and Deal Central with KBB ICO lets them see real numbers and real trade equity before they ever sit down."

Cox Automotive Forecast: October new-vehicle sales hold steady despite lower consumer sentiment, higher borrowing costs

Used SUV prices hit $25,000 for first time

The average used SUV in the CARFAX Used Car Index rose 0.7% in October to $25,125, the first time above $25K, and up 4.4% year over year. Vans and minivans also set a new high, while used hybrid and EV prices have slipped $650 since August even as hard-to-find RAV4 Hybrids sell well above new sticker.

What it means for us: Used SUV values are climbing while hybrid and EV values soften month to month. That's a live-market pricing problem vAuto Provision and Profit Time are built for, and a reason to watch aging on electrified units closely.
