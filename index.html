Study: Google car searches are fading as AI and social take over car shopping

A Cision and Brandwatch report looked at 251 million online auto mentions and found Google interest in terms like "best SUV" has been sliding since late 2024. Car conversation online rose 91% in a year. When the study ran 350+ "best car" prompts through AI tools, YouTube was cited in about 4,000 answers and Toyota's own site in just 135.

What it means for us: AI answers are built from video, reviews and listings more than the OEM site. That makes Dealer.com content, Autotrader and KBB listings, and DDC video and social the stuff that gets a store into the answer.

Talk track: "Your shoppers are asking ChatGPT and YouTube now, not just Google, so let's make sure your Dealer.com content and Autotrader listings are what those answers pull from."

LinkedIn angle: YouTube got cited about 4,000 times in AI car answers. Toyota's own website got cited 135 times. So what does that mean? Your website is not the front door anymore. It's one room in the house.

Ford, Honda, Stellantis and VW sue New Jersey over dealer warranty pay

Four automakers filed a federal suit on Nov 20 against New Jersey's law on warranty and recall repair pay, which took effect April 1. They say it moves more than $375 million a year from OEMs to dealers through labor multipliers and marked-up parts pricing. The state dealer association says it expects to win.

What it means for us: warranty pay is now a fight worth hundreds of millions, so clean warranty ROs matter. Xtime and Dealertrack DMS keep the labor time and parts on each warranty line documented. Expect dealers in states with similar laws to ask about it.

UVeye pitches the service lane as a one-minute appraisal tool

UVeye says its drive-through scanners are in 1,200+ locations and read about 4 million vehicles a month. Each report is ready about 30 seconds after the car rolls through. The scan pairs condition data with Carfax history and vAuto, so a used car manager can work up a trade value in about a minute, and dealers can set alerts by VIN, model or color.

What it means for us: this is the service drive as inventory source, sold by a partner that already plugs into vAuto. Pair it with Xtime appointments and KBB ICO offers and the "own auction block" story gets a lot easier to show.

Talk track: "Every car in your service lane is a possible trade, so let's get Xtime and vAuto working together and you see which ones to buy before the customer leaves."

CBT News · Nov 23, 2026

Training CEO: letting AI do the thinking will dull your sales team

Amplifire CEO Bob Burgin warns against an AI "easy button" in the showroom. Ready-made answers stop reps from building skills and hide mistakes, and newer staff are the least likely to catch confident errors. His pitch is AI that coaches with questions instead of handing over scripts.

What it means for us: dealers are going to ask whether AI replaces their people. The VinSolutions answer is AI that handles busy work, like Virtual Contact Assistant follow-up, with Performance Managers coaching the humans on the deals that need judgment.

Reuters: U.S. banks are putting their own AI agents on a shorter leash

Several big and mid-sized U.S. banks are tightening controls on their internal AI agents, according to unnamed sources. Steps include cutting off access to critical systems, limiting credentials, adding kill switches and keeping backup plans for loan processing and fraud checks. Banks had already blocked outside agents from customer accounts.

What it means for us: lenders are getting strict about AI agents, and that rolls downhill to dealers. Expect questions about how AI touches credit apps in Dealertrack F&I and Deal Central, and how much a dealer's AI tools can do without a human.
