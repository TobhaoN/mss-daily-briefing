Automotive Market

August new-vehicle pace tops 16M for sixth straight month

Cox Automotive's updated estimate puts August new-vehicle sales at a 16.8M SAAR — the strongest pace of 2026 and the longest streak above 16M since early 2020 — even as volume slipped about 5.8% year over year. Demand is being carried by affluent, high-credit buyers and strong Asian-brand and hybrid results, shrugging off tariffs and higher rates.

What it means for us: A resilient-but-thinning buyer pool is exactly the vAuto conversation — dealers need to turn inventory to the trims that actually move, and lean on vAuto's live market data rather than gut feel as YoY volume softens.

"The market's still running above 16 million, but it's the affluent buyer carrying it — let's make sure your vAuto stocking mirrors where that demand actually is."

Q2 auto finance data reflects the hybrid boom

Experian's Q2 State of the Automotive Finance Market report shows hybrids climbed to 16.8% of new-vehicle financing (up from 13% a year earlier) while EV share slipped to ~8.2%. Hybrids carry the lowest average monthly payment ($646 vs. $692 for EVs and $721 for gas), and several top hybrids show subvented rates well below the 6.4% industry average.

What it means for us: Payment-led shoppers are gravitating to hybrids — a cue to configure VinSolutions payment presentations and vAuto stocking around hybrid demand, since that's where the affordable-payment story lives right now.

Research claims 62% of dealer ad spend can't be traced to a sale

A self-published research paper surfaced by Auto Remarketing argues most dealership ad spend is untraceable to profit. Using an open-source "Banner-VIN-Profit" attribution model on 100 listings at one midsize franchise store, the author says 62% of ad spend "carried no defensible link to a sale or a dollar of profit" — framing untraced spend as trapped working capital.

What it means for us: Attribution anxiety is the opening for Dealer.com's first-party data and reporting — when dealers ask "which of my ad dollars actually sold a car," DDC's VIN-level attribution is the answer rather than a spreadsheet audit.
