⭐ TOP STORY

Toyota and Honda may get stuck with the bill for Trump's Canada tariffs

A proposed 50% U.S. tariff on Canadian-built vehicles — double the current 25% — lands hardest on Toyota and Honda, which together make more than three-quarters of Canada's cars. Analysts say Canadian-built vehicles were nearly a quarter of Honda's and 17% of Toyota's U.S. sales last year (RAV4, CR-V), and the levy could force some assembly lines to close if it takes effect January 1. Toyota and Honda may get stuck with the bill for Trump's Canada tariffs

Washington's proposed 50% tariff on Canadian car imports would fall disproportionately on Toyota and Honda, which dominate Canadian production. Both may have to shutter lines or redirect U.S.-bound RAV4 and CR-V output — a costly scramble given plants elsewhere are near capacity.

What it means for us: New-vehicle pricing and availability on high-volume imports could swing fast. That's a live-inventory question — lean dealers on vAuto for stocking and pricing agility and Manheim wholesale signals so they aren't caught flat-footed if RAV4/CR-V supply tightens.

Honda, Nissan target rollout of joint vehicle software in fiscal 2029

Honda and Nissan will jointly develop standardized electronic control units and software for software-defined vehicles, aiming for shared architecture from fiscal 2029; Mitsubishi may join. The move follows their abandoned merger and reflects pressure from software-rich Chinese EVs.

What it means for us: SDVs mean more OTA features and service revenue shifting toward software — a fixed-ops and retention story. Dealers will ask how they keep those customers; that's the Xtime service-retention and VinSolutions lifecycle conversation.

Dealership & Retail Tech

Why AI + analytics is the real winning formula for dealership websites

On CBT's Inside Automotive, Dealer.com and VinSolutions product leader Robert DeGeorge warns dealers not to treat confident AI answers as fact. LLMs summarize well but weren't built to measure website performance, and "garbage in, garbage out" applies when they lack real traffic and conversion data.

What it means for us: This is our opening to reframe AI-website hype around trusted first-party data. Position Dealer.com analytics and VinSolutions reporting as the verified data layer that AI recommendations should sit on top of — not be replaced by.

Talk track: "When a dealer says an AI tool told them their site is fine, ask what data it saw — Dealer.com's real traffic and conversion metrics are what should be driving those calls."

📣 POST-WORTHY UNCONFIRMED — contributed op-ed

AI agents need their own identity before they need a gateway

A security architect argues enterprises are moving from AI assistants to autonomous agents that reason, call APIs and chain tools — so authentication alone isn't enough. He calls for "runtime trust": continuously checking that an agent's actions still match user intent, with least-privilege access and human approval for high-risk steps.

What it means for us: As dealers deploy agentic AI on top of CRMs like VinSolutions and DMS platforms like Dealertrack, "who approved this action?" becomes the buying question. It's a differentiation angle for how our tools govern automated actions on customer and deal data.

Suggested LinkedIn angle: "In auto retail, the next AI question isn't 'can the agent do it?' — it's 'should it, and who signed off?' Runtime trust is about to matter as much in the CRM as it does in the SOC."
