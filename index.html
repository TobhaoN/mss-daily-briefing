TOP STORY · Automotive Market

Record car payments and 84-month loans raise affordability concerns

Edmunds head of insights Jessica Caldwell told CBT Live that the average new payment hit a record $787 in Q3, and about one in four buyers now finance for 84 months or more. More than 20% pay over $1,000 a month and down payments fell. She says the average buyer now pays about $10,000 in interest over a new loan, and lease penetration has dropped to about 19%, which shrinks future CPO supply.

What it means for us: Changes how we sell Dealertrack F&I and Deal Central. This lines up with our own Credit Availability Index, which showed record long terms. Dealers that structure honestly now keep customers out of the negative-equity trap at the next trade, and that's tomorrow's repeat business.

"Andrea Sells Cadillac" shares the social media habits that bring in buyers

Andrea Anderson of Andrews Cadillac in Brentwood, Tennessee, says social video became a steady sales channel after about a year of consistent posting, with early traction at three to six months. She now gets calls, messages and referrals every day and has sold to buyers in Arizona, Florida, Texas, Wyoming and Montana. She says real people beat polished promos and spends one to two hours a day shooting and posting.

What it means for us: Changes how we sell Dealer.com and VinSolutions. Shoppers want to meet the salesperson before they meet the store. Push video in VinSolutions follow-up and rep content on Dealer.com, and remind dealers this is a consistency play, not a viral one.

Tesla robotaxis accused of speeding in school zones as NHTSA weighs a probe

NHTSA opened a defect petition review on Oct. 9 covering about 1,000 2026 Tesla Robotaxis, including the Model Y and Cybercab, over alleged speeding in active school zones. The file lists four incidents and no crashes or injuries, and the agency has until Jan. 21, 2027, to decide on a formal investigation. NHTSA is separately pressing Tesla on how it certified the Cybercab, which has no steering wheel or pedals.

What it means for us: Autonomy is getting real scrutiny, which buys franchised dealers time. Dealers will ask whether robotaxis change service demand. For now it's a fleet story, and Xtime and fixed ops still own the customers who drive their own cars.
