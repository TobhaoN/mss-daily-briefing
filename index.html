TOP STORY · Competitor Watch

Federal judge sides with CDK Global on Tekion's monopoly claims

A federal judge granted CDK summary judgment on Tekion's monopolization and attempted monopolization claims, finding CDK's share of the U.S. franchise DMS market was below 50% in 2023 and 2024 whether counted by rooftops or revenue. The court also threw out Tekion's expert revenue-share estimate as unreliable. The order is partial, covering only those two antitrust counts, and the rest of the dispute between the two companies is still open.

What it means for us: A court just put on the record that no single DMS vendor dominates franchise rooftops, which quietly undercuts the "you're locked in, switch to us" pitch from Tekion and anyone else. Use it as air cover when a dealer says the DMS market is a two-horse race, and keep the Dealertrack DMS conversation on integration depth and conversion timelines instead of market-share theater.

Talk track: "A judge just found nobody owns half the franchise DMS market. So the question isn't who's biggest, it's whose integrations actually move your deal from the desk into the DMS without re-keying."

New-vehicle inventory builds again in October as listing prices top $50,000

Available new inventory hit 2.92 million units in October, up 4.2% from September for a second straight month of growth, with days supply rising to 87. The average listing price reached $50,498, the highest in the dataset. The spread by brand is enormous: Toyota sits at 39 days supply and Lexus 45, while Jeep is at 159, Ram 148 and Ford 112. Units priced over $60K carry roughly 106 days of supply versus 74 days in the $30K–$40K band.

What it means for us: Stores with Jeep, Ram or Ford franchises are the ones feeling this, and they will ask what to price down first. That is a vAuto Live Market View and Provision conversation about the $60K-plus units aging on the lot, paired with Dealer.com merchandising so the expensive metal actually gets seen.

Talk track: "Your $60K-and-up units are carrying about 106 days of supply industry-wide. Let's look in vAuto at which of yours are on that pace and what a price move does to your turn."

United Airlines blocks AI agents from booking flights, citing cybersecurity risks

United will block AI agents and autonomous assistants from booking flights or managing reservations for customers, citing cybersecurity risk and conflicts with federal rules that require a human to complete check-in. It is restricting access on its own platforms and through travel agencies and aggregators. The move follows Bank of America warning customers not to share banking passwords with AI assistants.

What it means for us: Big-ticket retailers are drawing a line on letting AI agents transact, which is exactly the question coming for digital retailing. Dealers will ask whether an AI agent can submit a credit app or structure a deal, and the answer for Deal Central and Dealertrack is that the consumer still authenticates and consents themselves, which is a feature not a limitation.
