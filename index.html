TOP STORY · Cox Automotive Ecosystem

Cox Automotive confirms VinSolutions cyber incident, says activity contained

Cox said on Oct. 26 that it detected suspicious activity tied to VinSolutions, contained it quickly, and is working with outside cyber experts to determine what data was involved. Cox says no encrypting malware was involved, dealers could keep using VinSolutions, and no other Cox platform was affected. It believes a limited subset of dealers was affected and will notify them directly. A hacking group posted claims on Oct. 23, but the user and password counts circulating online are unconfirmed. CBT News reports that law firms are exploring a possible class action.

What it means for us: Expect VinSolutions dealers to raise this on calls this week. Stick to Cox's public statement and any internal guidance, don't speculate on scope or data, and send "is my store affected?" questions to the dealer's Cox representative. This is not a talking point in either direction.

Holding line: "Cox has said the activity was contained, VinSolutions kept running normally, and any affected dealers will be contacted directly. Let me connect you with the right person on our side for anything specific to your store."

Automotive Market

Used SUV prices hit $25,000 for first time

In the CARFAX Used Car Index, the average used SUV rose 0.7% in October to $25,125, its first time above $25,000 and up 4.4% from a year ago. Vans and minivans also topped $24,000. Used hybrid and EV prices have fallen $650 since August to $33,470 but are still up 6% year over year. Used RAV4 Hybrids average $49,000, above new starting prices, because the redesigned model is hard to find. Overall used prices barely moved.

What it means for us: SUV pricing is now a precision game. vAuto price-to-market shows which SUVs on the lot are under- or over-priced, and Xtime plus KBB Instant Cash Offer can pull hot units like RAV4 Hybrids out of the service drive before they reach auction.

Talk track: "Used SUVs just crossed $25,000 and RAV4 Hybrids are selling above new sticker, so let's use vAuto to see which units you're leaving money on and who in your service drive is driving one."

Dealership Marketing & Advertising

David Spisak says dealers must audit for AI, launches new show on CBT News

David Spisak, CEO of Disruptive Growth Solutions, says shoppers now ask AI tools for advice before they visit a dealer site, and some research and buy through platforms like ChatGPT and Instagram without visiting one at all. His team's audits find dealer sites missing the schemas and data connections AI search needs, along with multiple phone numbers, no inventory connection and weak vehicle descriptions. Those gaps can lead AI agents to skip a store. He says websites should work as the engine that feeds AI search.

What it means for us: His audit list doubles as a Dealer.com discovery checklist: one phone number, a live inventory feed, and strong vehicle descriptions from vAuto merchandising. It builds on Friday's Cox piece on AI discoverability.

Talk track: "Let's check your site against what AI tools look for: one phone number, live inventory, and real vehicle descriptions. If any of those are missing, the AI skips you."
