Chinese car sales in Mexico surge despite new tariffs

📣 Post-Worthy

Chinese-brand sales in Mexico rose nearly 30% in H1 to 137,525 units — 17% of the market — even after a 50% tariff took effect in January. Officials counter that imports actually fell 43%, arguing brands are drawing down front-loaded inventory. It's a live case study in how tariffs reshape flow more than shelf presence.

What it means for us: A vivid data point that used- and new-inventory mix is shifting under tariff pressure — exactly the volatility vAuto Live Market View and Manheim valuations help dealers price around when a segment suddenly floods or dries up.

Reuters — July 20, 2026

China's car market heads for worst year since 2021 as sales fall 20%

Passenger-vehicle sales in China fell 20.2% in H1, prompting the CPCA to cut its full-year forecast to a 14% decline. Rising fuel costs and pulled-back EV subsidies gutted ICE demand (ICE retail down 39% in June), squeezing margins to ~3.4% and pointing to a shakeout to seven or eight major players by 2030.

What it means for us: Overcapacity abroad plus razor-thin margins is what feeds the export wave and pricing pressure US dealers eventually feel — reinforcing the KBB/Manheim affordability narrative we already use to frame trade and acquisition conversations.
