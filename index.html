Ford F-150 production halted at 2 plants amid parts shortage

Ford paused F-150 production at plants in Michigan and Missouri after a supplier disruption limited parts availability for its top-selling pickup. The stoppage threatens near-term supply of one of the highest-demand vehicles in the country.

What it means for us: A pinch on F-150 supply pushes used-truck values up, so this is the week to coach dealers to use vAuto and Manheim data to acquire and price pre-owned pickups aggressively before the shortage tightens the lane.

Talk track: "If new F-150s get scarce, your used truck inventory is suddenly worth more — let's set your vAuto acquisition strategy now while Manheim values are still catching up."

Nissan to launch hybrid Rogue in US in November

Nissan's Americas chairman said the Rogue e-Power hybrid arrives in the US in November, targeting hybrids to reach 30–40% of its mid-size car sales. Nissan is also raising US localization to 80% by 2030 to blunt tariff exposure.

What it means for us: A fresh mainstream hybrid entry expands the "electrified but not full-EV" shelf, so make sure Dealer.com VDPs and Autotrader/KBB merchandising are ready to capture hybrid-intent shoppers the day allocations land.

Automakers seek lifeline from defence boom

Ford, GM and JLR are bidding on military vehicle contracts and selling idle plants to defense firms as car demand slows and Chinese competition intensifies. Analysts say the revenue impact is modest — GM Defense is ~$700M this year against $185B in group revenue.

What it means for us: The real signal is excess capacity and margin pressure at the OEMs, which means more incentive volatility ahead — a reason to keep dealers close on vAuto stocking discipline rather than chasing thin new-car grosses.
