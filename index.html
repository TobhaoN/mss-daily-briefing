TOP STORY · Cox Automotive Ecosystem

Auto credit access rises for a fifth straight month as long loan terms hit a record

The Dealertrack Credit Availability Index rose 1.9% to 107.1 in September, its highest level since April 2015 and 9.4% above a year ago. Approval rates climbed to 73.9% and subprime share rose to 17.4%. A record 31.8% of loans ran longer than 72 months, negative equity rose to 57.8% and down payments slipped to 12.8%. All Used, Independent Used and Franchised Used each set series highs.

What it means for us: Changes how we sell Dealertrack F&I and Deal Central. Lenders are saying yes, but deals are getting stretched. Stores that structure smart, with the right term, products and trade equity, will keep customers from being stuck underwater at the next trade cycle.

Talk track: "Our Dealertrack data says credit is the easiest it's been since 2015, so let's use Deal Central and Dealertrack F&I to get more buyers approved without stretching them past what they can trade out of later."

Automotive Market

KBB: new-vehicle prices flirt with an all-time high

Kelley Blue Book puts September's average new transaction price at $50,562, just $52 below the record set last December. The year-over-year gain was under 1%, well short of a normal September, because more buyers shifted to subcompact SUVs, compact cars and smaller pickups. Incentives averaged $3,359, down 8.8% from a year ago, and the EV price premium over gas models has held near 11% all year.

What it means for us: Changes how we sell vAuto and Dealer.com. Buyers are trading down in segment, not walking away. Stores should stock and merchandise the affordable segments hard and price used units against that $50K ceiling.

Talk track: "KBB has new prices sitting right at the record, so let's make sure vAuto has your used compact SUVs priced to catch every buyer who just got sticker shock on new."

Hyundai dealer's secret to selling more than 600 vehicles a month

Hyundai of New Port Richey sold 606 vehicles in September. Store manager Parth Donda says the store stopped wholesaling used cars entirely and sold about 40 vehicles under $5,000 last month. Those cheap deals don't make much, but they build word of mouth and bring families back for lease and CPO. Used sales went from about 1,700 in 2024 to 2,800 last year, with 3,000-plus expected this year.

What it means for us: This is the vAuto story in one store. Retail every trade, recon fast with iRecon and use Xtime to win back the fixed-ops business going to quick lubes, which is exactly Donda's 2027 goal.

Talk track: "A Florida Hyundai store hit 606 a month by retailing every trade, so let's look at what you wholesaled last month in vAuto and what that cost you in future customers."

LinkedIn angle: 606 cars in a month. No secret tech. Same team for five years and they stopped wholesaling. Here's the kicker. Consistency beats talent and it beats software too.
