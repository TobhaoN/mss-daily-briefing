NADA: hybrids now 15.7% of sales and turn twice as fast as gas cars

NADA's Market Beat put October SAAR at 15.54 million, down 0.9%, with the year on pace to top 16 million. Hybrids hit 15.7% of sales year to date while EVs slipped to 6.2%. Hybrids turned in about 26 days versus 53 for gas models, and the average new payment rose to $823 at a 6.81% rate.

What it means for us: hybrids are the fastest-turning unit on the lot. vAuto Provision and Stockwave help dealers price and source used hybrids before the market catches up, and KBB ICO pulls them in through the service lane.

Talk track: "Hybrids are turning in about half the days of your gas units, so let's use vAuto to find every used hybrid you can buy before the street does."

Toyota raises full-year profit forecast as U.S. hybrid demand holds

Toyota's July to September net profit fell 19% to 759.4 billion yen, but it raised its full-year profit forecast to 3.57 trillion yen. Quarterly sales rose to 13.4 trillion yen despite U.S. tariffs and higher material costs. Camry and RAV4 hybrids led U.S. demand while China sales weakened.

What it means for us: Toyota stores are flush and short on hybrids. That's a vAuto conversation about sourcing late-model used hybrids and an Xtime conversation about owning the service visits on all those hybrids already on the road.

Most service teams can't explain effective labor rate, and it shows in profit

eDealer Solutions founder Jen Suzuki says a posted $210 labor rate can sit far above what the shop actually collects after discounts, warranty, internal work and overrides. She names four habits that drag it down: instant discounts, manager overrides, weak tech notes and inconsistent quotes. Her fix is teaching advisors to explain the repair before the price.

What it means for us: ELR is a process problem we can help with. Xtime Inspect puts photos and tech notes in front of the customer, and consistent menu pricing in Xtime takes the guesswork out of quotes.
