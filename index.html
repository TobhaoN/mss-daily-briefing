Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index: Mid-October 2026 Trends

Wholesale values firmed in the first half of October, with the Manheim index up 0.7% from September to 207.4 and 2.2% above last year. Cox's Jonathan Gregory says leaner auction supply, not stronger dealer buying, slowed depreciation, and much of the annual gain comes from an easy comparison with a weak October 2025. EV values slipped 0.7% this month as more off-lease EVs arrive, while compact cars led the segments at 5.6% over last year.

What it means for us: Dealers will ask whether now is the time to load up at auction. Supply is tight and demand is flat, so the smarter play is sourcing what's already theirs: vAuto for appraisal and pricing discipline, and the service drive as their own auction block through Xtime. Soft pickups and slipping EV values also mean aged-unit pricing in vAuto matters more than usual this month.

Talk track: "Auction supply is tight and nobody's paying up, so your cheapest used car this week is probably already sitting in your service drive."

GM raises free cash flow outlook, announces $20 billion buyback

GM beat third-quarter estimates with $3.70 in adjusted earnings per share on $50.0 billion in revenue, raised its 2026 free cash flow outlook to $11.5 to $12.5 billion and authorized a new $20 billion buyback. North America produced $3.6 billion of its $4.1 billion adjusted EBIT on trucks and SUVs, even as U.S. sales declined. Redesigned 2027 Silverado and Sierra pickups are next, with Super Cruise going standard on high-end trims.

U.S.-Mexico trade talks reportedly stall over auto tariffs, raising concerns for USMCA

Reports say U.S.-Mexico trade talks have stalled after Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick refused to lower 25% tariffs on cars and 50% duties on metals, though officials on both sides deny the talks are stuck. CarGurus faces NYC lawsuit over used-car deal ratings and hidden fees

New York City's consumer protection agency says CarGurus put "Great Deal" labels on listings that only looked cheap because mandatory fees and conditions sat in the fine print, citing more than 2,000 violations. Its lead example is a used CR-V shown about $2,200 under market that ended up roughly $1,500 over once fees were added. CarGurus rejects the claims and says it will fight.

What it means for us: Dealers will keep asking whether their own listings carry the same risk. The fix lives upstream of any marketplace badge, so walk them through how fees flow from vAuto pricing into Dealer.com VDPs and Autotrader listings.
